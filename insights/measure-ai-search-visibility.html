AI search measurement

How to measure AI search visibility for a B2B website

Measure AI search visibility with three kinds of evidence. Check whether your pages appear in AI answers, whether those answers send people to your site, and whether the people you hear from are a fit. A small B2B team can do this with Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, its web analytics and CRM, and a short list of questions it checks regularly.

By Mike Cubberly, founder of Bottle Rocket Growth · November 13, 2026

Start with the search engines’ own reports

Google Search Console has dedicated Generative AI performance reports for Search and Discover. The Search report shows impressions for your URLs in features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode. Review which pages appeared and compare dates, countries, or devices when the breakdown is useful. Google says the data also remains in overall performance reporting, so adding the dedicated impressions to the overall total would double-count them. Google Search Central’s report guide

Bing Webmaster Tools’ AI Performance report counts citations across supported experiences, including Microsoft Copilot and AI-generated summaries in Bing. It shows cited pages and a sample of the phrases the system used to find sources. A grounding phrase can help you understand why a page surfaced. It may differ from the question the person actually asked. If Bing shows Topics Portfolio for your site, use it to see which groups of questions are producing citations. Bing’s AI Performance documentation · Bing’s Topics Portfolio documentation

Check a repeatable sample of buyer questions

Start with 10 to 15 questions your buyers ask before they contact you. Include a few about what your company does, several about problems you solve, and some that reflect how a buyer compares options. For a firm that helps with conferences, “How do I verify a B2B conference attendee list?” tests something different from “Who can help me book meetings at a B2B conference?”

Run the same questions on the same surfaces each month. Start a fresh conversation for each question where possible. Record the exact prompt, surface, date, region, and any relevant settings. For each answer, note whether it names the company and whether it links to a page you own. A third-party article about you can explain a mention, so save that URL separately. Keep results where the engine gives no AI answer or supplies no citation. Otherwise the success rate can go up just because the denominator went down.

If two of 12 checks mention you and one links to your guide, record two mentions and one owned-site citation across 12 checks. Those counts describe the questions and answers you sampled. They can help you choose a page to improve or a fact to clarify.

Use one tracking sheet with distinct measures

Measure Where to get it What to record each month
Google AI impressions Search Console Generative AI report Date range, Search or Discover, impressions, and top URLs
Bing AI citations Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance Date range, citation count, and top cited URLs
Sampled mentions and citations Your repeated prompt checks Checks run, answers naming the brand, answers linking to an owned URL, and source links
Identifiable referral visits Web analytics Sessions with a known AI referrer, landing page, and date range
Qualified inquiries Intake form and CRM Account fit, stated need, recorded referrer, buyer-reported discovery source, and owner

Ask “How did you first hear about us?” on your form or first call. Someone may see your name in an AI answer and type it into a search engine later. Keep their answer alongside the analytics referrer. If both point to AI search, count one inquiry and retain both pieces of evidence. Use the same qualification criteria you apply to other inbound leads.

Use the review to choose what to fix

If an answer gets your service wrong, check the company description across your homepage, service pages, and public profiles. If a guide earns impressions or citations and you see few relevant visits or inquiries, check whether it answers a question your buyers ask and gives interested readers a next step. Mark major edits and compare the same windows next month. A technical audit can surface crawlability, structure, and content issues.
