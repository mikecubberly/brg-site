Event research

How to research a conference audience using public sources

Start with three pages from the event organizer. The agenda tells you what happens and where. The speaker lineup identifies people with a published role. The sponsor directory identifies companies with a published relationship to the event. Put each observation in its own field. You’ll know which conversations you can prepare, which companies to research, and where you still need someone to confirm their plans.

By Mike Cubberly, founder of Bottle Rocket Growth · November 13, 2026

What AWS re:Invent’s public pages show

We checked the public AWS re:Invent 2026 agenda, keynote lineup, featured speakers page, and sponsor page on November 13, 2026. The examples below show how a team can use those pages to plan its own outreach. Reopen the sources before scheduling because the program can change.

Source What we observed on November 13 How to use it
AWS re:Invent agenda The agenda lists a welcome reception in the Expo at The Venetian on Monday, November 30, from 4 to 7 p.m. local time. Set aside a planning window at that venue. Record a contact’s participation only after they confirm their plans.
AWS re:Invent keynote lineup The Wednesday Data & AI Keynote describes getting agents into production, with reliable access to data and clear limits. It names scheduled speakers. Use the topic to prepare a specific question for a listed speaker. Check the program and the person’s availability before suggesting a meeting.
AWS re:Invent featured speakers The page shows selected speakers with their roles and links to the session catalog. Record the published speaker role. Open the relevant program entry to capture the session and format if available.
AWS re:Invent sponsors AWS identifies an Emerald sponsor tier and lists companies in that group. Record sponsorship at the company level. Leave the individual participation field unknown until a separate source supports it.

Add account fit before naming people

Suppose a team helps engineering groups set up approval steps for AI agents. The Data & AI Keynote gives that team a concrete conversation topic. A target account still needs a reason to belong on the list. The company might run an active AI program, and the relevant role might own platform governance or production operations. Check both before asking someone to meet.

If the organizer lists a sponsor that fits, first check whether your team already knows someone at that company. An existing relationship owner can ask who plans to be at the event and whether the topic is relevant. For a published speaker, the session topic can make the message specific even if you propose a conversation after the event.

Match the invite to the evidence

These fictional notes show how the same topic can lead to two different asks. Use the first with a person whose role in the program you’ve verified. Use the second with an existing contact at a listed sponsor when you still need to confirm who’s attending.

Published speaker

I saw you’re listed for the Data & AI Keynote. I’m curious how teams decide which actions an agent can take once it’s in production. If that’s something you’re working on, would you be open to comparing notes for 15 minutes after re:Invent?

Company sponsorship only

I saw your company is sponsoring re:Invent. Do you know who from the platform team is planning to be there? We’re comparing approaches to approving agent actions in production. If it’s relevant, I’d be glad to make time during the week or catch up afterward.

Leave a record someone else can verify

For each row, record the company, why it fits, the person and their current role if verified, the event edition, what the source actually establishes, its URL, and the date checked. Add an owner and a next action. If the person confirms a conversation, update the meeting record with the place or call link, local time, and agreed topic. Preserve the original evidence so the next person can tell what changed.

Start with a handful of accounts. Have the people doing the outreach check the sources before sending anything and tighten the notes wherever they have to guess. Verify your conference attendee list · Plan meetings at AWS re:Invent · Turn conference conversations into pipeline
