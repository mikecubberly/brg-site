How to choose your first AI workflow for B2B sales

Choose a sales task your team already repeats and can check quickly. Account research is a good place to start. Give the workflow a small set of named accounts and one question, then have it draft a source-backed record for a human to review. A short review queue lets you see whether the work saves time and improves decisions before you connect it to the rest of your sales process.

By Mike Cubberly, founder of Bottle Rocket Growth · November 13, 2026

Pick one question an account owner can act on

Start with a specific question, such as “Which of these accounts has announced a new distribution site in the last 90 days?” The answer helps an account owner decide what to research next. Agree on what counts as evidence and which sources are acceptable. For example, a dated company announcement or a statement from a local economic development agency might be enough to verify the announcement. A search snippet alone may only point you toward a source you still need to open.

Give the workflow the company’s name and domain. Tell it to flag uncertainty about the company, date, or project. If an account shares its name with another business, the reviewer should be able to see which one the source describes. Include an option for “No evidence found in the sources checked.” That status helps the owner understand what the research covered and decide whether a second pass is worth doing.

Give the reviewer the evidence and the possible implication

Design the record so someone can check it in a minute or two. Put the observed fact beside the source, then give the inference its own field. Here’s a fictional record for a team that sells warehouse deployment services.

Field Example entry
Account and question Sample Distributor A · Has it announced a new distribution site in the last 90 days?
Observed fact The company published an announcement last month about a new distribution site and said it expected to open next spring.
Source and dates Dated company announcement · Store the original URL and the date reviewed in a live record.
Possible implication The project may involve an equipment installation or relocation.
Open questions Who owns the project? Has an installation partner been selected? Is the opening date still current?
Suggested next action Ask the account owner to verify project scope and the relevant role.
Review status Pending review

Ask the reviewer to open the source, confirm it refers to the right account, and check the quoted or paraphrased claim against the original. They can accept the record, correct it, request more research, or close it. A recent site announcement supports further investigation. The sales team still needs to learn whether there’s work to win and who makes that decision.

Keep the first pilot inside the research queue

Choose a batch the team can review in one sitting. Include ordinary accounts alongside ambiguous company names, old announcements, and accounts where you expect limited evidence. Agree on which sources the workflow can read and which information the team needs to store. Give the account owner the review decision, with access to the evidence and a way to record corrections. Keep messages, account assignments, and opportunity changes in the team’s existing process while you test the research.

When a reviewer edits a record, capture the reason. The most useful notes name the error, such as “announcement is outside the date window” or “source describes a different company with the same name.” If the same correction keeps coming up, tighten the research instructions or add a clear review check before running another batch.

Compare the pilot with the work you do today

Time a comparable batch using the current process. For the AI-assisted batch, count the time spent preparing inputs, checking sources, fixing records, and handing the work off. Compare batches with a similar mix of straightforward and difficult accounts.

Measure What to track
Time per usable record Total time spent on the batch divided by the records the team can use
First-pass acceptance Records accepted as written divided by records reviewed
Material errors Wrong companies, dates, or unsupported claims found during review
Action taken Records that led an account owner to a specific research or sales next step

Review the results with the people who did the work. If they spend most of their time untangling identities or checking vague sources, fix that step first. If the records consistently stand up to review and save time, try a larger batch using the same question and review rules.
