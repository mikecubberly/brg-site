How to book meetings at AWS re:Invent 2026

AWS re:Invent 2026 runs November 30 through December 4 in Las Vegas. To book meetings that are worth taking, start with one reason to talk, verify who has a connection to the event, and offer two short meeting windows near where you’ll already be. Put the venue in the invite as soon as both sides agree. Official AWS re:Invent 2026 site.

By Mike Cubberly, founder of Bottle Rocket Growth · November 13, 2026

Pick the conversation before you pick the name

Start with ten accounts you’d want to talk to even if the conference were a month away. Include current customers, open opportunities, and potential partners your team can genuinely help. Give each account one owner and pick the person who can move the conversation forward.

If you want to discuss Start with Give them a reason to meet
Migration or developer productivity The platform or infrastructure lead A trade-off you can help them work through, such as how to phase a migration without delaying releases.
Cloud cost or spend visibility The FinOps owner or engineering leader responsible for spend A practical question, such as who owns the gap between usage reporting and team budgets.
A partner opportunity The alliances or Marketplace lead A named customer segment you both serve and a specific next step you could take together.

Match the invite to what you can verify

Use the official catalog to find named speakers by topic. Use the sponsor directory to identify companies represented at the event. A company listing establishes that the company is sponsoring. Confirm who from the company will be in Las Vegas through a public announcement or a direct reply. Record the source and use language that matches what it says. AWS session catalog · AWS sponsor directory.

For a named speaker, reference their published session and ask one question tied to it. For a sponsor where you have a relationship, ask your contact who from their team will be there. When you have a strong fit and attendance is unknown, ask directly and give them an easy way to continue after the show. For example, an invite from someone attending might read like this.

Are you heading to re:Invent? We’re comparing how platform teams assign ownership for shared cloud costs. I’d be up for swapping notes for 15 minutes near The Venetian on Wednesday at 11:30 a.m. or 2 p.m. Pacific. If Vegas is packed, we can catch up the following week.

Book by venue and leave travel time

Build your calendar around the sessions you care about and group meetings by venue for each half day. AWS suggests allowing 30 to 45 minutes between venues. If you’re planning booth visits, the official agenda lists The Venetian Expo welcome reception on Monday evening and Expo hours Tuesday through Thursday. Check the app before moving across the Strip because shuttle times and stops can change. AWS agenda · AWS travel and scheduling guide.

Put Pacific time, the property name, a specific meeting point, and one line on the topic in the invite. For a booth meeting, include the booth number. If two colleagues will attend from your side, agree on who will open the conversation and who will capture the next step.

Follow up while the conversation is fresh

Write down one detail you heard, anything you promised, and who owns the next step. Send a short note the same day or next business day with the promised link, introduction, or proposed time. If the meeting fell through, offer a short call after the event and mention the topic that earned the original yes.

At the end of the week, count meetings held and follow-up steps agreed. That gives you a concrete starting point when deciding which accounts to pursue after Las Vegas. For help putting the outreach and handoffs together, see Bottle Rocket Growth’s conference meeting booking service.
