How to choose a LinkedIn outbound agency

Choose a LinkedIn outbound agency by looking at who it would contact, who would represent your company, and what happens when someone replies. Ask each agency to show you a small sample of accounts, walk through a reply from the first message to the sales handoff, and spell out what its fee covers. You’ll learn more from that conversation than from a slide full of reply rates.

By Mike Cubberly, founder of Bottle Rocket Growth · November 13, 2026

Decide what you actually need help with

Keep outbound in-house if your team already knows the buyer, can write credibly to that market, and has someone who can own campaigns and replies every week. You’ll keep the feedback close to sales. Give that person time for list review and follow-up as well as sending.

Bring in an agency if you know which problem you solve but need someone to build the audience, test the message, and run the work consistently. If your sellers want to handle every conversation themselves, look for an agency that can set up the campaigns and hand over replies. If replies regularly go unanswered, look for managed reply handling and name the person on your team who will take qualified conversations from there.

If the buyer or offer is still up in the air, start with a small research and messaging test. Pick one segment, agree on what you want to learn, and decide what would justify expanding it. Check your ideal customer profile before anyone starts building a list.

Ask the same questions on every agency call

Use the same offer and target segment when you brief each agency. That gives you something concrete to compare.

Ask What to look for
Show me five accounts you’d start with. Why these five? A clear reason each company fits, a relevant role, and a source for any claim the message will make.
Which accounts would you leave out? Existing customers, active opportunities, do-not-contact records, and companies that fall outside the agreed segment.
Whose profile would a buyer see? A real, authorized representative with an accurate affiliation and a clear relationship to your company.
Who answers if a buyer asks a question? A named owner, a response window, and a path to someone who can handle a technical or commercial question.
What would make us pause or change the test? A review date and concrete decisions about the audience, message, or handoff based on what the team learns.
What do we get back at the end? An agreed export of campaign data, contact history, exclusions, and open conversations your team needs to pick up.

Compare fees against meetings your team actually holds

Ask for the total cost over the same pilot period. If a quote is per sender, multiply the fee by the number of senders and months. Add setup and any research, reply handling, or reporting billed separately. For an in-house comparison, include the time your team spends researching accounts, running the campaign, and managing replies.

Agree on what makes a meeting qualified before launch. A useful definition includes the account and role you want to reach, plus a relevant reason to talk. Record bookings, cancellations, and no-shows separately. Cost per held, qualified meeting equals total pilot cost divided by the number of held, qualified meetings. If none have happened, show the spend and the zero meeting count. If an agency quotes a reply rate, ask whether the denominator is invitations sent, connections accepted, or unique prospects messaged. Use the same denominator when you compare rates.

Have sales record which meetings became accepted opportunities. It’s easier to decide whether to keep spending when you can trace each meeting to what happened after the call.

Put sender ownership and the handoff in writing

Before launch, ask who owns each profile, who authorizes access, and who can pause activity. Check what the sender’s profile says about your company and how the agency handles LinkedIn’s rules on account use and automation. LinkedIn User Agreement.

Have the agency show you what sales receives when a buyer is ready to talk. A useful handoff includes who the person is, why the team reached out, what the buyer actually said, any commitments made, and who owns the next step. Do one dry run so a rep can pick up the conversation without asking the prospect to repeat it.

Agree on how you’ll get the contact records, source notes, approved messages, reply history, and do-not-contact updates back at the end. Decide who will finish open conversations and how access will be removed. Ask for a sample export while you can still use it to decide who to hire.
