HeyReach sequence architecture: context, handoffs, and measurement

A practical framework for reviewing HeyReach workflows, personalization, reply ownership, exclusions, and pipeline measurement without universal benchmarks.

Mike Cubberly · Originally published April 24, 2026 · Revised September 8, 2026

Every client is different. These articles explain approaches we may use, not a checklist applied to every engagement. Research depth, tools, channels, team support, and deliverables depend on the client's goals, market, existing systems, budget, and agreed scope. Examples are not performance guarantees.

A sequence is a workflow, not a growth strategy by itself. Before configuring steps, establish the audience, offer, sender, reason for contact, and who will handle the conversation. If those decisions are weak, adding more follow-ups will not resolve them.

Bottle Rocket Growth (BRG) can help design the research, message logic, operating process, and measurement around outreach. HeyReach is one tool that may be relevant. Neither the tool nor the example structure below is a standard requirement for every client.

Separate product capability from platform permission

HeyReach documents actions including connection requests, an “If Connection” condition, messages, delays, and sequence endings. Its connection condition distinguishes existing connections from people who are not connected. That is not the same as automatically interpreting whether a reply is interested, negative, or an objection. Source: HeyReach action-step documentation.

LinkedIn prohibits unauthorized automated activity. Vendor features do not override the platform's rules. Review the channel and account risks before choosing a workflow; no sending schedule in this article should be read as a guarantee against restrictions. Source: LinkedIn prohibited-software policy.

Give each step a reason to exist

An illustrative conversation structure is an introduction, one useful follow-up, and a clear close when there is no engagement. It is not a prescribed number of steps. A warm introduction may need no sequence; an existing conversation should be handled in its own context.

  • Opening: explain the relevant reason for reaching out without pretending to know an unverified internal problem.
  • Follow-up: add useful context or evidence instead of repeating the same ask.
  • Close: leave a respectful next step and stop when continuing is no longer appropriate.

Write for readability rather than an invented universal character target. Confirm the current limits for the selected account and message type. Choose timing around the audience and response capacity, then evaluate it using comparable groups rather than a blanket “best day” rule.

Test the handoff, not just the message

Inspect personalized fields and fallback copy before use. Missing names, incorrect company references, stale roles, or an unsubstantiated signal can undermine the entire message. Check both relevant paths through the sequence and confirm that exclusions are applied before contact.

Assign a reply owner and define what should happen for interest, an objection, an existing relationship, or a do-not-contact request. Confirm actual stop behavior across connected systems; do not assume a CRM change stops a separate campaign.

For example, HeyReach's HubSpot documentation says deleting a HubSpot contact does not remove that lead from HeyReach campaigns. Integrations require explicit configuration and verification. Source: HeyReach HubSpot integration documentation.

Measure the full path with clear denominators

Acceptance
Accepted invitations divided by invitations sent, with a defined observation window.
Positive replies
People expressing relevant interest divided by unique people contacted. Define “positive” before comparing cohorts; do not count every non-negative reply as intent.
Meetings held
Completed meetings divided by unique people contacted. Report bookings and no-shows separately.
Qualified opportunities and revenue
Track agreed qualification criteria, opportunity progression, wins, and revenue over an appropriate sales-cycle window.

Keep counts beside percentages. Distinguish contact-level metrics from account-level outcomes, and avoid counting the same person twice across senders or channels. Response volume alone does not show that the team is building sustainable pipeline.

Improve the weakest part of the process

Review targeting, evidence quality, offer, message, timing, reply handling, and sales follow-through together. Change one meaningful variable at a time where practical. No acceptance rate, meeting yield, or response-time target in a generic article can substitute for the client's own baseline, sample size, and economics.
