Event audience research

How to verify a B2B conference attendee list

A conference attendee list is ready for outreach when you can explain three things about each person you plan to contact. Does their company fit? Have you identified the right person in their current role? What connects them to this specific event? Keep those answers in separate fields. The same person can be a great prospect, an exact identity match, and a complete unknown for attendance.

By Mike Cubberly, founder of Bottle Rocket Growth · November 13, 2026

Decide who belongs before you research attendance

Write down the companies you want to meet, the roles that matter, and the conversations you want to have. A team selling equipment deployment might want facilities or operations leaders at multi-site businesses. A team recruiting implementation partners might look for practice leaders at consultancies. Those are different lists even when the teams are going to the same show.

Include the account owner and any exclusions before research starts. If an account executive already has a conversation going, a conference invitation can come from that person. If your team has agreed to leave an account or contact alone, carry that decision into the event list.

Match each source to the claim it supports

A sponsor directory is a starting point for company research. An event agenda can identify speakers. A dated post from a person saying they will be there can support a direct invitation. Record the source, what it actually says, and when you checked it. Verify the event name, year, and location before treating an old page as evidence for the upcoming edition.

What you found What to record Useful next action
The person confirms directly that they plan to attend Attendance confirmed by the person, with the date Invite them to meet around a relevant topic
The organizer lists the person on this year's agenda Published speaker, with the session and source Tie the invitation to the topic or session
A roster you're permitted to use shows a named registrant Registered, with the roster date Verify role and ask whether they have time to meet
The company is listed as a sponsor and the person works there Company is sponsoring, individual attendance unknown Ask who from the team plans to attend
An old roster lists the person at last year's event Prior-year attendee, current attendance unknown Look for a current source before assuming plans

Registration captures a plan at a point in time. After the event, use a direct meeting, conversation, or appropriately shared check-in record if you need to record actual attendance. Keep a badge scan, a held meeting, and a qualified opportunity as separate outcomes.

Resolve partial names and titles before exporting

Suppose a roster contains only a company and the title “Director of Operations.” Check whether the company name refers to the parent, a division, or a business with a similar name. Then check current roles. If two plausible directors remain, mark the identity unresolved and keep the row out of your outreach list. A working email address tells you that the address can receive mail. It tells you very little about whether that person matches the roster row.

Here's a fictional example. A conference roster shows “Regional Operations Director” at a multi-site operator. You find two people with matching titles in different regions. One publishes a post saying she is speaking at the event, and the organizer's agenda names her. You can record her as a published speaker and check whether her region matches your target. The original roster row can stay unresolved until you have enough evidence to tie it to her.

Build a review sheet that survives a handoff

Start with the fields below and add only what the team needs to act.

Field What goes in it
Company and account fit Company name, domain, and a short reason it belongs
Person and current role Name, title, profile or source, and match status
Attendance basis Direct confirmation, published speaker, registered, company-only evidence, or unknown
Source and check date Where the evidence came from, what it supports, and when you reviewed it
Owner and next action Who will invite, confirm plans, resolve the match, or leave the row out

Review a sample before the full list goes out. Ask another person to open the evidence and explain why each row is ready. If they need the researcher in the room to figure it out, improve the notes. Before launch, deduplicate by person and company, recheck exclusions, and flag stale evidence. Hand over the list with enough context that an account owner can open any row and write a sensible first message.
