§01 / Why Events

Registration is a declaration.

Most intent signals are inferred. Event attendance is voluntary, public, and time-bound. When a buyer registers, they have already done four things you would otherwise spend months trying to read between the lines on.

01
Topic commitment
They opted into a specific subject, vendor ecosystem, or category. The category is the qualifier.
02
Budget posture
Travel approved, time blocked, ticket paid. The internal sign-off has already happened.
03
Time-bound window
There is a before, during, and after. Each has a different optimal message.
04
High-attention mode
During the event, attendees are on their phones, on LinkedIn, between sessions. Reply rates rise.

§04 / Process

Five steps from event to inbox.

The mechanics are tight enough to run a campaign in under two weeks for most events, including same-week launches if the event is already underway.

  1. Name the event & the slice
    You tell us the event and your target audience. We confirm the contact volume, the timeline, and which delivery model fits.
  2. List built within 48 hours
    We pull from public attendee data, exhibitor lists, session speakers, and signal sources. Fit-scored, priority-tiered, ready to review.
  3. Strategy & copy approved
    For DFY and IOY, we propose the sequence, the sender voice, and the message variants. You sign off before anything ships.
  4. Outreach runs
    DFY: we execute. IOY: your team runs the playbook through your profiles. Data: we hand over the file. All paths kick off the same week.
  5. Reporting & reuse
    Acceptance, reply, and meeting metrics by sender and segment. The list and the playbook are reusable for the next event in the calendar.
  6. Iterate or stand down
    After the event window, decide: retainer the motion across the next quarter's events, or hold for the next conference your team cares about.
§05 / Proof

A recent mid-cycle test.

Numbers from a recent event campaign launched after the conference had already started. The cycle was already in motion when outreach began, which compresses the working window and biases attendee attention toward LinkedIn during sessions.

One reply across the run came back as "I'm not attending." Every other response engaged with the topic. The combination of relevance (registered attendee) and timing (mid-event attention) pulled response rates well above what cold outbound typically clears.
Caveat worth stating: events drive elevated LinkedIn activity, so mid-event windows are easier than pre- or post-event. Pre-event windows still outperform topic-only cold lists, but expect the spread to narrow.
